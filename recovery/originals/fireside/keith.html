Was Elmer Keith a Benchrest Shooter?

By John Del Savio

 

It was on an evening in February a few years back while at the Pine Tree Rifle Club when the topic of Elmer Keith came up. Several club members were gather in the club’s pub area  after some informal (indoors range) pistol practice, and the conversations was the expected of firearms, hunting and so on. I remember that evening well as it was snowing heavy and the warmth of the club house fire place was very comforting. One of the Members made mention of deer hunting with a revolver in 44 magnum. One thing lead to another about the 44 magnum and its inventor, Elmer Keith.

 

When I made mention that Elmer Keith was once at the Pine Tree Rifle Club back in the 1950’s, I was asked  “Was Elmer Keith a Benchrest shooter?”. I did hear that he was at the PTRC, but I didn’t know if he was a BR Shooter. I set forth to find out.

 

Not many of today’s handgun enthusiasts under the age of 50 may know who Elmer Keith was, but he was a well know writer back in the mid 20th century and the Father of the 44 Remington Magnum cartridge. Elmer wrote of hunting with hand guns and was always an advocate of large diameter heavy bullets, in both handguns and rifles. Elmer didn’t just write about them he used then and knew about what he wrote first hand.

 

That question sparked my interest.  I started an investigation with a call to a good friend who was a Benchrest Shooter back in the early 1950’s at the PTRC. Wally Hart of Hart Rifle in Nescopeck, PA was present at nearly all of the early BR matches that took place at the PTRC. When I asked Wally if he remember Elmer Keith being a BR shooter he said no, however he did remember Elmer being at the PTRC once. Wally told me that he was passing through and stopped to see what was going on at the match.

 

Elmer Keith lived in Salmon, Idaho and Johnstown, New York was more that a hop, skip, and a pistol shot from his home. So now there was a new question to find an answer for, why was Elmer passing through Johnstown.

 

Recently I acquired an old group photograph take at the PTRC back in 1955 that is proof positive that Elmer was indeed at the PTRC. In the photo below he can be seen standing 3rd in from the right in the second row. That’s Elmer fore sure with both his cigar and Stetson hat.

 


 

 

 

In the photo below a close up of that end of the group photo shows not only Elmer Keith clearly but some others that are quite notable.



 

Front row at the left is Olive Walker, her daughter, and husband Mike Walker with the sun glasses on.  Mike Walker is the inventor or the .222 Remington and designer or the Remington 700 rifle, as well as much more of what made Remington’s rifles famous for accuracy.

 

Standing in the 4th row with ball caps on and eye glasses are Bob Hart and son,  a very young Wally Hart.

 

In the third row down from the top on the far right with the striped shirt is Sonny Starin, and to the left of his is Frank Hubbard one of the founding fathers of modern Benchrest shooting.

 

Getting back to why Elmer Keith was at the PTRC has been discovered. It was in 1955 that Remington Arms, and Smith & Wesson announced their joint effort and introduced the 44Remington Magnum cartridge in the new Smith & Wesson model 29 as the most powerful handgun in the world. Elmer Keith had just left the Remington Arm Plant at Illion, New York and was on his way to the Smith & Wesson in

In Springfield, Mass. Working on the joint project. Having a friend in Johnstown, NY Elmer stopped to stay the night with his friend. His friend told him of the PTRC and that there was an important rifle match being held and the two went to visit. They just happened to be there when the group photo was taken. So as Paul Harvey would say, “Now you know the rest of the story“.
